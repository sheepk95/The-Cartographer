# Examples

The positive examples are original compositions following the documented first-touch structure, validated against a published commission-pitch example from a creator-platform vendor. The negative example is an original composition assembled from the sourced negative signals (bad subject lines, documented mistakes) and annotated line by line. In a real engagement every fact comes from the interview; never invent numbers, posts, or familiarity.

## Positive - B2C, commission offer, direct email

Scenario (illustrative):

- Brand: a home-coffee-gear brand pitching a vetted creator whose channel reviews budget espresso setups.
- Offer: already decided at 12% commission, 30-day attribution, monthly payout.
- Contact: creator lists a business email.
- CTA the brand can honor: sample on reply.

> Subject: your $300 espresso setup video - and our grinder
>
> Hi Marisol,
>
> Your "$300 espresso setup" video is the most honest budget build I've seen - and people setting up their first machine at home are exactly who buys our hand grinder.
>
> I run partnerships at [brand]. We just opened our creator program: 12% commission on every sale through your link, tracked for 30 days, paid monthly. On our average order that's about $11 per sale.
>
> If you're curious, just reply and I'll send a grinder over so you can try it before deciding anything.
>
> Dana Okafor, Partnerships, [brand]

Why it passes the gate:

- Subject names her actual work.
- Opener is a verifiable specific framed as audience fit, not flattery.
- Commission mechanics front-loaded exactly as the model requires: rate, window, payout, per-sale estimate. Vague "great commission" language is the documented failure.
- One low-friction sample-on-reply CTA.
- No rate card, no deliverable list, no contract talk.
- Fails the swap test in the right direction: no other creator fits this message.

## Positive - B2B, flat fee, via listed contact

Scenario (illustrative):

- Brand: an incident-management SaaS pitching a vetted SRE-newsletter writer (18k subscribers, mostly on-call engineers).
- Offer: flat-fee sponsored deep-dive, budget confirmed at $2,500.
- Contact: she lists a sponsorship email.
- CTA: reply for the details doc.

> Subject: sponsored deep-dive for On-Call Weekly
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> Your postmortem-culture series reads like it was written for our users - engineers who own the pager and hate their current alerting stack. That overlap is why I'm writing.
>
> We'd like to sponsor one deep-dive issue: your voice, your angle, our tool as the worked example. Paid flat fee - we've budgeted $2,500 for this - and we know a sponsored issue only works if it's genuinely useful to your readers.
>
> Want the one-page details? Just reply and I'll send it over.
>
> Sam Reyes, Partnerships, [product]

B2B notes: the pitch speaks to a business need of her audience (on-call pain), not just a product feature - the one consistently documented B2B-specific rule.

- The fee is named (budget signal, no rate-card dump).
- The deliverable stays a one-line sketch with the detail doc gated behind the reply.

## Follow-up plan (fits either example)

| Touch | Day | Channel                | Angle           | Summary                                                                                                                                            |
| ----- | --- | ---------------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | 0   | Email (listed contact) | Fit + offer     | First touch above                                                                                                                                  |
| 2     | 7   | Same thread            | Held-back proof | Two sentences: one conversion or audience proof point not in touch 1, re-offer the CTA                                                             |
| 3     | 21  | Same thread            | Easier ask      | Two sentences: shrink the ask (sample only / details doc only), then close: "if the timing's off, no pressure - I'll leave it here." Then honor it |

Positive reply at any touch: stop, hand off to negotiation with the stated numbers unchanged. Silence after touch 3: close the file; reopen only with something genuinely new.

## Negative - the pitch that gets deleted

Original composite of the sourced negative signals, annotated line by line.

> **Subject: Collab opportunity with GlowCore!! 👀**

Both documented bad-subject patterns at once - the generic "collab opportunity" template and the emoji-bait "quick question" energy. Brand-first, creator-absent.

> **Dear influencer,**

The documented generic salutation. Two words in, the mail-merge is visible.

> **I hope this message finds you well! I've been a huge fan of your amazing content for a long time.**

Filler opener plus fabricated familiarity - no post named, because none was watched. Unverifiable flattery where the audience-fit reason should be.

> **GlowCore is a revolutionary clean-beauty platform with 4 product lines, AI-matched shades, dermatologist partnerships, and over 40,000 five-star reviews.**

Feature dump. Nothing answers "why my audience?" - the personalization principle every source agrees on is missing at the exact spot it belongs.

> **We offer very competitive compensation. Our full rate card and 12-week campaign plan with 9 deliverables across 3 platforms are attached, and rates are non-negotiable.**

Three documented leave-outs stacked:

- Vague compensation where a real number belongs.
- The rate-card/campaign-proposal dump.
- Aggressive negotiation language ("non-negotiable") in message one.

> **Please also note all content will require our standard exclusivity terms and FTC disclosure language, per the attached contract.**

Contract, exclusivity, and disclosure terms in a cold pitch - every documented workflow places these after agreement. Here they read as handcuffs before hello.

> **Sign the contract here, follow us on Instagram, and book an onboarding call this week!**

Three asks, none low-friction. The one-ask rule is unanimous across outreach guidance.

> **Best regards,
> The GlowCore Growth Team**

No named human. A team alias forecloses the reply-to-a-person CTA that the entire pitch should funnel into.

Net effect: every documented negative signal in under 130 words - swap-test-proof that it was written for no one. The two positive examples fix each line structurally.
