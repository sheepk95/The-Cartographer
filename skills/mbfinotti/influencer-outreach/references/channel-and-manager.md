# Channel choice and manager etiquette

## Which channel carries the pitch

Pitch the contact the creator publishes for business: check the bio link, media kit, and about page first, before applying any ranking below.

- A DM blast to a creator who lists a partnerships email signals the sender never looked.
- A cold email scraped for a DM-only creator signals the same.

The ranking below decides only the residual cases: the creator publishes more than one route, or publishes none.

Default order, highest value per unit of effort first (re-rank against the Interview answers):

1. **The listed manager or agent.** Effort: an hour, the same grounding and personalization a direct pitch needs, plus relationship capital you keep spending afterwards. Value: a decision from someone who evaluates offers for a living and whose yes carries the creator - and a relationship that pays again across their whole roster.
2. **The listed business email.** Effort: an hour, grounding plus the full message structure. Value: the channel the creator opened for business, where a complete offer fits and the thread stays followable.
3. **A DM.** Effort: near-zero. Value: lowest - it lands beside fan mail, and the 2-4 sentence no-links limit means a commission offer's mechanics barely fit.
   - Counter-signal, single-practitioner: brand-side practitioner Agita Matule (marketing team at a delivery platform) still prefers DMs - "less formal, and a lot of influencers don't list their email." This marks DM-first as a legitimate style, not a mistake.

Where the axes disagree:

- efficiency: manager > business email > DM
- value: manager > business email > DM
- effort, highest first: manager == business email > DM - a genuine tie: both need the same hour of grounding before a word is written, and the manager's extra cost is relationship capital rather than drafting time.
- compliance cost, highest first: DM > business email > manager
  - DM: violates a platform's unsolicited-commercial-message rule, an account-level action appealable only through the platform.
  - Cold email: triggers the unsolicited-contact regime (identify the sender, honor an opt-out, hold a lawful basis for the address in some jurisdictions), reversible by suppressing it.
  - Manager: a published listing is an invitation to pitch, so it triggers nothing.

Delete, never demote, the option ruled out: drop the DM row entirely once the creator publishes an email or a manager - a DM parked at the bottom reappears as a second parallel touch, which is the end-run failure below. Drop the manager row only when the creator has personally asked for direct contact.

What this order starves: the small creator who publishes neither an email nor a manager. The efficiency order pushes every hour toward the two top rows, so those creators get skipped, and they are often the ones whose reply rate is highest and whose rates are lowest.

Promote the DM whenever no business contact is published, or the creator's own bio invites DMs. Vendor survey direction supports the default: a "47.7% email-first vs. 6.8% DM-first" split, vendor-published with undisclosed methodology - use the direction, not the numbers.

**B2B**: channel choice at the pitch stage has no B2B-specific convention to inherit, so the same gate and the same order apply.

## When a manager or agent is listed

The ranking above already puts the manager first; this is the etiquette that makes that row work.

- **Treat the manager relationship as a relationship, not a gate to slip past.** Address the manager, name the creator, state the fit evidence and the offer plainly; managers filter on economics fast.
- **Never go around a listed manager unprompted.** An end-run reads as disrespect to both manager and creator and can end the conversation before it starts.
- **A counter-signal exists, and it is now corroborated**: consultant Jason Falls reports (2026) that about half a dozen creators asked him to contact them directly rather than through their talent manager, and that an informal poll of his peers found the same pattern - his own account is still single-caseload and an undisclosed-size peer poll. A named survey now backs the same direction: HypeAuditor's Influencer Outreach Survey found 67% of influencers who work with agents or talent agencies still prefer to be contacted directly by brands, and only 15% of influencers use an agent at all.
  - Read it as: if a creator _tells you_ to contact them directly, honor that preference without hesitation.
  - Never read it as license to bypass a manager on your own initiative.
- If the manager goes silent, follow up with the manager per the standard cadence. Do not open a parallel direct thread to the creator while the manager thread is live.

## Recording the choice

Log the channel used and whether a manager was in the loop alongside each pitch in the outreach log - channel is one of the variables the manager's own reply-rate history will eventually rank.
